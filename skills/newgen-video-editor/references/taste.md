# What Jerome likes and rejects

From the memory `user-likes-dislikes` and the review notes, 5-17 Nov 2026, grouped by topic. Check every plan and export against this list, and add new likes and rejects here in the same turn (with the date and client). A client tag in brackets means the note came from that job. Treat it as the default elsewhere unless that client's memory says otherwise.

## Working style
- He hates: forgotten directions, blaming his approval, repeated mistakes, going quiet, long waits, many small rounds ("Tarunga ra imung trabaho, ga cge ratag balik2"), and opening Facebook or other sites without saying so.
- "Let's minimize the revisions." Before sending, watch the changed section at 12 fps and the whole film at 3 fps, re-check every earlier note, and compare transitions with the last approved version frame by frame.
- He wants the cut on the timeline early, so he can comment at a frame.
- When asked to "enhance", change only what he asked for, and list any extras so he can veto them. [Tacoma: a spotlight behind Dave was rejected]
- Don't add disclaimers the brief lacks (an "AI-generated presenter" line was rejected).

## Picture and realism
- Likes: real motion (the presenter walking and gesturing, the camera moving, background people working, laughing and pointing). Cinematic camera moves: push-ins, rack focus, light flares. Hyper-realistic, Adam-level production. An excellent first 2 seconds.
- Rejects: standing talking heads, empty streets, locked-off shots (even when a brief says so), still crowds holding phones, people walking backwards, soft or blurry foreground people, model-perfect "animated" faces and glamorous crowds, static photo inserts, and drone or high-wide openers (the face gets small and breaks; open with a push-in instead).
- Real footage jobs: a stock or AI look is an instant client reject. Fewer, truer B-roll shots beat a shot on every word. B-roll fills the frame (no blurred letterbox) and never shows unrelated content.
- Real people on camera beat documents. Flag privacy risks; don't remove people on your own. [Pura]
- A presenter must speak on camera with real lip-sync, never a still photo under the voice. [Fora]
- Social posts and phones: real-looking feeds, the real website composited on the phone. Props get a designed app-style UI with icons, not plain words. [LO2] For "browsing a site", use real hands scrolling a phone, not a laptop with a desktop page forced on it.

## Captions and text
- Every spoken line gets a caption (for deaf viewers), except where a headline or graphic already states the words, or where it would crowd the face. [Dave 06, Tacoma, 4Runner]
- Clean, on the word, word for word with the script, including its case. Start about 0.16 s before the word so keywords can be read. Icons are centred over the caption.
- Ai Videos Co style: one word at a time on the chest, from the reference styles only (orange, white, yellow glow, white glow, big yellow); small orange italic with a hard black outline for connecting words. Pill captions were rejected as plain.
- Rejects: soft caption shadows, dirty or overlapping captions, thick outlines on a face seam, stacked caption stages ("your" then "YOUR TRUCKS"), wandering positions, templated name labels, tiny script or URL text, and text over highlights (hands, handshakes, mascots, products).
- Hook headline: keyword in CAPS, big, in the brand colour, boxed, at the top, clear of the face. A headline banner on the opening line is welcome ("20 Years in Sales").
- [A4 v2 only] He tried a calligraphy script with glow plus a bold sans with a drop shadow; this overrode the no-shadow rule for that job.

## Graphics and motion
- Likes: motion infographics on the beat (labelled 3D callouts, check/cross cards, counters, trackers, meters, VS columns, stamps). Full-feature highlight rectangles held through the shot (no small circles). Real brand logos for named features (JBL, Apple CarPlay). A brand-logo reveal when a brand is named. A chrome shine (light sweep and glint) on badges. A gold check or pop when a choice is made. Emoji graphics for food ideas. Subtle soft-gold keyword highlights, the same way every time.
- Likes in transitions: light flash and light sweep, a glitch on a big scene change, object drive-by transitions, a fast bold water ripple on frame 1 (about 1 s, no sound). [Fora]
- Rejects: a different gimmick per keyword ("SLOP"), whiteboards, icons and stickers, small cards over footage, generic motion graphics, clip-path wipes, swirl or fake phone screens, broken stripe bars, a glow on the product, and a full-screen black card replacing the video.
- Transitions: soft and clean (short dissolves or light flashes, a 0.45-0.6 s crossfade). Rejects: too fast or back to back, a speaker flashing for a split second between cutaways (give about 1 s of the speaker first), dissolves on internal AI cuts, and visible hard cuts where the client style hides them. [Travis: every cut needs a transition]
- Keep the AI clips' own timing, cuts and lip-sync. Mute a line rather than cut picture that breaks the scene timing. [Fora]

## Logos and end cards
- Every video gets its own logo ending. Likes: logo with light (flare, glow, shine, stacked build; keep the glow subtle), the vehicle driving forward to reveal the logo, the presenter leaning on the vehicle, grounded at true scale with contact and floor shadows.
- The end card must wait for the speaker to finish, never cuts the presenter, has real motion (not static), and has the logo on top with the headline in the middle.
- Rejects: a plain text end card, a freeze then cut to black, a white logo on a bright sky (put light logos on a solid brand card).
- Use the client's real logo and badge (for example from their PDF), and say where it came from.

## Sound and music
- Likes: music matched to energy and occasion, new for every video (an anniversary gets party music; a lively speaker gets upbeat pop at about 125-130 BPM). "Good music, I'm excited" for the stomp-clap rock. A drama score under dialogue. Soft SFX: a "ting" on card pops, an engine rev on the end card (the real vehicle's engine). [Brightwater] whooshes on graphics.
- Rejects: soft, weak or reused music; music too loud (keep it about 9 dB under the voice); birds or calm ambience on a busy street; frog-like chopped horns or growls; falling sub sweeps; hissy noise bursts; cricket clicks and wind under the voice; a water-drop sound on a ripple; stitched sound bites that don't read as full sentences; a clipped next word at a join.
- Ai Videos Co: no whooshes (except Brightwater, above).
